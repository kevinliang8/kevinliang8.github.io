Kevin Liang

PhD Candidate, Department of Linguistics, UCLA
kevinliang8@ucla.edu | CV | Google Scholar

I am a PhD candidate in Linguistics at UCLA. I study phonology and language acquisition, mostly through experiments with infants, children, and adults. I also love anything to do with Catalan.

My dissertation, The Learnability of “Unnatural” Phonology: Experimental Evidence from Catalan, asks whether learners acquire sound patterns that phonological theory treats as unnatural, specifically saltation and opacity. My dissertation combines wug tests with Catalan-speaking adults and looking-time experiments with Catalan-learning two-year-olds. My committee is Megha Sundara (co-chair), Bruce Hayes (co-chair), Victoria Mateu, and Kie Zuraw.

I also work on how infants find morphemes and how they handle phonetic variation in words. My MA thesis, advised by Megha Sundara, Laurel Perkins, and Bruce Hayes, showed that phonotactics constrains how 6-month-olds discover suffixes. Before UCLA, I studied Linguistics and Computer Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I worked with Charles Yang on a computational model of syntactic category learning.

In January 2027, I will join the Department of Linguistics and Languages at McMaster University.
